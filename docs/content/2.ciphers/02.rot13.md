---
title: ROT-13
description: A Caesar with shift 13, so it is its own inverse. Encode twice and you are back.
---

::cipher-facts{name="rot13"}
::

Thirteen is half of 26, so shifting twice is a full turn. `encode` and `decode` do the same thing, and `info().selfInverse` admits it. One key, hides nothing. It's the Usenet way of keeping a spoiler out of immediate view, and that is all it was ever for.

```ts
const rot13 = create("rot13");
rot13.encode("HELLO").text; // "URYYB"
rot13.encode("URYYB").text; // "HELLO"
rot13.encode("Hello, World! 123").text; // "Uryyb, Jbeyq! 123"
```

Letters only. Digits and punctuation pass through, and that is the whole difference from [ROT-47](/ciphers/rot47).
